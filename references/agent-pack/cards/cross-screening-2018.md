# Cross-screening in observational studies

Zhao, Small, and Rosenbaum (2018), *Cross-Screening in Observational Studies
That Test Many Hypotheses*, JASA 113, 1070–1084.
DOI: 10.1080/01621459.2017.1407770. Relevant PDF pages: 2–4.

The method uses one part of the observations to choose hypotheses, alternative
directions, and analyses for another part, then reverses the roles. Its motivation
includes the interaction between multiplicity and sensitivity analysis for
nonrandom treatment assignment. Selection can concentrate testing on effects that
have a chance of surviving both burdens. This is not a general argument that
screening always improves discovery yield.

The original two-way arrangement and allocation of error across analyses must not
be copied without matching the actual design. The current task supplies its own
testing level and an independent target subgroup. You do not have access to the
other groups' observations or permission to pool them.

The paper distinguishes a global-null discovery from concordant findings across
parts. A confirmatory rejection in one target alone does not establish replication.
The observational sensitivity analysis is application-specific; neither screening
nor multiplicity adjustment is a substitute for assumptions about an estimand or
causal identification.
