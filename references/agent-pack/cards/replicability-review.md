# Orientation to replicability analysis

Bogomolov and Heller (2023), *Replicability Across Multiple Studies*, Statistical
Science 38, 602–620. DOI: 10.1214/23-STS892. Relevant PDF pages: 1–2 and 12.

This review distinguishes an effect in at least one study from evidence of an
effect in several studies. A meta-analytic discovery can be driven by one study
and need not be replicable. Directional replicability additionally accounts for
which direction is supported across studies. Partial-conjunction hypotheses make
the required number of studies explicit.

The review describes methods for single and multiple features, including
frequentist and empirical-Bayes approaches, and discusses assumptions and power
limitations. Its summaries do not replace the conditions of the original methods.
Directional analyses must account for how directions and families were chosen.

For the current task, use this distinction to describe what your target analysis
does and does not establish. A fixed design chosen elsewhere can support target
confirmation under its assumptions. Replicability across subgroups requires a
separate, specified combination of the confirmatory evidence, not an inference
from the strength of the source subgroup's exploratory results.
